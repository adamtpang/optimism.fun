# Choose Good Quests (Trae Stephens and Markie Wagner)

Reference note, read 2026-11-07. This is a summary in our own words, not the essay.
Read the original:

- Founders Fund, 2023-06-22: https://foundersfund.com/2023/06/choose-good-quests/
- First ran on Pirate Wires, 2022-11-23: https://www.piratewires.com/p/choose-good-quests

Authors: Trae Stephens (Founders Fund partner, Anduril cofounder) and Markie Wagner
(founder of Delphi Labs).

## The argument

1. **A quest is the objective you center your life on.** Some have an end state
   (publish a book), some do not (maximize net worth).
2. **Good versus bad.** A good quest makes the future better than today. A bad quest
   barely improves the world, or makes it worse.
3. **Hard versus easy.** Hard quests are high risk, operationally complex, and usually
   fail (reverse aging, Mars, cure cancer, AGI, a new country). Easy quests have a known
   playbook. Most remaining good quests are hard: hard to make profitable, R&D heavy, or
   near-impossible technically. So anyone optimizing for "easy" mostly lands on easy and bad.
4. **The player card.** You are a player with skills, resources, and powers (energy,
   network, money, management experience, social capital). Whether a quest is achievable
   depends on the quest and the player together. For a player with no fit, skipping a hard
   quest costs little and attempting it can be the worse choice. For a player with rare fit,
   skipping it is a large loss.
5. **The traps after a first success.** Becoming an investor in a crowded, zero-sum market
   for allocation; becoming a public commentator instead of building; copying a proven
   product to take market share. The Facebook alumni network is the cautionary case; the
   PayPal network is the contrast.
6. **The moral claim.** For the most capable and best resourced, choosing a bad quest is
   not neutral but a loss for humanity, because hard quests are nearly impossible for
   everyone else.
7. **Good examples named:** Musk, Branson and Bezos in space; Altman at OpenAI; Luckey at
   Anduril; Reinhardt at Charm Industrial; Kaditz at Q Bio.
8. **Quests that need high-level players:** semiconductor manufacturing, industrial
   automation, resource discovery, next-generation energy, low-cost construction, new
   transport, general AI, brain mapping and interfaces, extending lifespan.
9. **The closing question:** are you on a good quest now? Be honest.

The essay's own chart puts quests on two axes (good to bad, easy to hard). It adds no
scoring method. Our numbers are our own and should be read as estimates, not as the
essay's verdict.

## Limits worth keeping in mind

- It is written for already-successful founders with money and social capital. For a
  player without those, the essay itself says a far-fetched hard quest can be the bad choice.
- "Good" is asserted, not measured. Ranking quests still needs evidence.
